Nights out in the cab: what to pack

Last updated October 2026

A week of nights out is much easier with the right kit. The aim is simple: sleep well, eat properly without spending a fortune at the services, and stay clean and safe.

Fatigue is a factor in many serious truck crashes, so good sleep is a safety issue, not a luxury. Block out noise and light, keep the bunk warm (or cool), and lock yourself in at night.

These amounts assume around 3 litres of water a day and two meals a day from home, kept in a 24V cool box. Take more water in hot weather or if you're handballing loads.

Stay safe at night

Park in a secure, well-lit truck park where you can, especially with high-value loads. Lock the doors, use a cab door strap, and if you run a diesel night heater, fit a carbon monoxide alarm near the bunk.

What to pack

  1. Sleeping bag or bunk duvet

    A proper night's sleep is the best safety equipment you can own.

    For day runs: 1 sleeping bag
    For a week of nights out: 1 sleeping bag

    Fatigue is a factor in many serious truck crashes. A warm, comfortable bunk makes a real difference to how rested you are, especially in winter if you want to save fuel by not running the night heater all night. A duvet in a stuff sack packs away during the day.

    • A rectangular bag that opens out into a duvet for milder nights
    • Rated comfortable to around 0°C if you sleep without the heater
    • Washable, with a cover or liner you can swap

  4. 12/24V electric cool box

    Bring food from home and save £10–£15 a day on truck stop meals.

Frequently asked questions

What do truck drivers need for nights out?

Bedding (a sleeping bag or duvet and a pillow), an eye mask and ear plugs, a 24V cool box and kettle, a flask, a wash bag and towel, shower flip-flops and something to secure the cab doors at night.

Can I run a microwave in my truck?

Most cabs can't run a microwave without a large inverter and extra batteries. A 24V lunch box heater or kettle is a simpler, safer way to have hot food.

Is it safe to use a night heater while sleeping?

Diesel night heaters are designed for it and are safe when properly installed and maintained. Have it serviced, never block the exhaust, and fit a carbon monoxide alarm by the bunk.
